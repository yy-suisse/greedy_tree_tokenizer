\section{Introduction}
[background]
1. EHR research arise nowaday, with it caracteristics of large amount of data routinely collected from health care. EHR can be used to train Foundation Model (FM), with potential to be assistive clinical decision tool
2. they often use standarized clinical vocabularies such as ICD, RXNORM, SNOMED, LONIC
3. standarized clinical vocabularies /ontologies DAG, are used to normalize clinical naming, and improve the interoperability
4. SNOMED CT is a DAG, comprehensive 400k + concepts (covering very broad clinical domains, diorders, medication, morphologies etc...) and semantically rich (composed by 100+ relationship types, have their own way to compose machine readable semantic expression), mappable to other clincial terminologies.

[Gap]
1.1 training FM needs either large amount of patient data (but patient data is difficult to get)
1.2 or need adequate size of clinical vocabularies. (but medical vocabularies are large size)

2. Current methods are:
2.1: (cite) LLM byte-pair on categorical medical concept: data-driven, no semantic preservation, highly dependant to data quality and cpncept orders.
2.2: (cite) keep highest prevalent top k concepts, highest entropy contribution top k concepts: data-driven, loss of considerable clinical information.
2.3: (cite) troncate/reduce leaf concepts to their parents concepts: data-driven, loss of medical granularity.
2.4: (cite) or just keep all concepts as it is, if we have lots of patient data. but since concept usage distribution are usually long tail, many rare concepts can not be learnt in a proper way.
2.5: (cite) learnable embedding space codebook: loss of controillability and interpretability
all of above mentioned methods are ignoring the semantic richness of clinical vocabulary DAG when try to deal with vocabulary size.

[Our method and contribution:]
We purpose a method:
1. applicable to DAG knowledge graph.
2. tokens selection: Represent concepts by smaller vocabulary size using greedy token selection approach
3. way to tokenize: Represent concepts by a tree/subgraph preserving the structure of the concepts the semantic value.
4. By apply to SNOMED CT concepts used in our local institut real data, we show our method can control how we tokenize and with what we tokenize (controllability and interpretability)
5. (maybe) if any mapping inter-ontology exist, we can use concepts in 1 ontology to represent/tokenize concepts in the other ontologies (interoperability)
