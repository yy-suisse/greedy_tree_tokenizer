The limitations above point to four directions for extending this work.

\subsection{Inter-ontology tokenization}

\begin{quote}\small\itshape
\begin{itemize}
\item if mppinf inter ontology snomed and others, can show case that we can tokenize concepts in 1 ontology with other concept tokens in other ontology
\end{itemize}
\end{quote}

Where a mapping between SNOMED~CT and another ontology exists, the same
selection and tokenization mechanism could be applied across ontologies:
tokens selected in one vocabulary could represent concepts from another,
extending the interoperability argument from Section~\ref{sec:introduction}
to the token level itself. \notetoself{this is not claimed as a contribution
of the current paper (see Section~\ref{sec:introduction}) -- it lives here
only, as you asked.}

\subsection{Redundancy and token swaps}

\notetoself{pulled from your PDF's \S8 "Perspectives" -- not in main.tex's
outline, but already sketched there.}

A second direction addresses a property of the greedy procedure itself
rather than of the ontology. The greedy algorithm of Section~\ref{sec:method:selection} never removes a
token once selected. A token can become nearly redundant once later tokens
end up covering the same concepts it was originally selected for; a
marginal-\emph{loss}-based pass could identify and swap out such tokens
after the fact.

\subsection{Primitive vs. fully-defined concepts}

\notetoself{also pulled from your PDF's \S8.}

A third direction questions an assumption made throughout
Section~\ref{sec:method}: that expanding a concept is lossless. Expanding a concept via its outgoing relationships
(Section~\ref{sec:method:tokenizer}) implicitly assumes that doing so is
lossless. For primitive SNOMED~CT concepts -- those not fully defined by
their stated relationships -- this may not hold. A reconstructibility factor
$\alpha(u) \in [0,1]$ ($\alpha(u)=1$ for fully defined concepts,
$\alpha(u)=\eta < 1$ for primitive ones) could discount the propagated
coverage of Eq.~\eqref{eq:coverage} accordingly, while still treating a
primitive concept selected \emph{as its own token} as fully covered
(coverage $1$), since it is then preserved atomically rather than
reconstructed from its relationships.

\subsection{Combining with an ontology-similarity measure}

\notetoself{also pulled from your PDF's \S8, which already cites a reference
for this ([6] in your PDF).}

A fourth, more speculative direction would change the objective itself
rather than build on top of it. An independently developed ontology-based similarity measure
\citeneeded{ontology-based similarity measure referenced in your PDF's
Section 8 / reference [6]} could evaluate how well semantic neighborhoods are
preserved and how severe token collisions are, and could potentially be
folded into the marginal-gain computation itself, e.g.\ as a
facility-location-style term -- provided the submodularity and monotonicity
properties the greedy guarantee depends on
(Section~\ref{sec:method:selection}) are re-verified for the modified
objective.
